The '''1999–2000 [[Argentine Primera División]]''' was the 109th season of top-flight [[football in Argentina]]. The season ran from August 7, 1999 to July 17, 2000.<ref name=rsssf>[https://www.rsssf.org/tablesa/arg00.html Argentina 1999–2000] by Javier Roimiser on RSSSF.com</ref> [[ Instituto Atlético Central Córdoba|Instituto de Córdoba]] (champion of [[1998–99 Primera B Nacional]]) and [[Chacarita Juniors]] (winner of "Torneo Reducido" after beating [[Juventud Antoniana]] in a two-legged series)<ref>"Argentina: 1ra. "B" Nacional AFA 1998/99 - Torneo Reducido por el 2do. Ascenso" by José Carluccio, 2 Dec 2012</ref> were promoted from Primera B Nacional.

As Conmebol extended the number of [[2000 Copa Libertadores]] clubs from 23 to 34, four Argentine clubs were eligible to play the tournament. As Boca Juniors and River Plate had already qualified, [[Rosario Central]] and [[San Lorenzo de Almagro|San Lorenzo]] (2nd and 4th respectively) earned their right to play the Copa Libertadores. On the other hand, [[Talleres de Córdoba]] replaced Gimnasia y Esgrima to play the [[1999 Copa CONMEBOL|1999 Copa Conmebol]] after the club from [[La Plata]] declined to participate.

At the end of Torneo Clausura, the best five placed teams earned right to play the [[2001 Copa Libertadores]] while six participants of [[2000 Copa Mercosur]] were eligible by similar system.

[[Club Atlético River Plate|River Plate]] won both, Apertura and Clausura championships (totalising 31 league titles to date). For the first time in Primera División, a [[promotion and relegation]] system was introduced.<ref>[https://www.infobae.com/2012/07/01/656565-la-promocion-una-definicion-dramatica-que-se-despide-del-futbol-argentino/ La Promoción, una definición dramática que se despide del fútbol argentino], Infobae, 1 Jul 2012</ref> The two teams with the worst average were directly relegated to the second division while teams placed 17th and 18th in average played two leg series each with two teams from [[Primera B Nacional]].

As a result, three teams were relegated, [[Ferro Carril Oeste]], [[Gimnasia y Esgrima de Jujuy|Gimnasia y Esgrima (J)]] (worst averages) and [[Instituto Atlético Central Córdoba|Instituto (C)]] (lost promotion to [[Club Almagro|Almagro]]). Belgrano remained in Primera after the series vs [[Quilmes Atlético Club|Quilmes]] ended 4–4 on aggregate.

=== Promotion Playoffs ===
For the first time in Primera División, a [[promotion and relegation|promotion playoff]] system was implemented in order to decide which teams would be promoted from the second division (or relegated from Primera División), apart from the two worst averages that were directly relegated. The system ruled that clubs with the third and four worst averages played a [[two-legged tie|two-legged]] series versus two teams qualified from [[Primera B Nacional]]. In case of being tied on points, teams in Primera División would win the series.

[[Instituto Atlético Central Córdoba|Instituto]] and [[Club Atlético Belgrano|Belgrano]] (both from Córdoba) played the [[promotion and relegation|promotion]] playoff v [[Club Almagro|Almagro]] and [[Quilmes Atlético Club|Quilmes]] (qualified from [[1999–2000 Primera B Nacional]]) respectively.<ref>"Argentina: 1ra. División AFA - Promoción 2000" by José Carluccio, 8 Jan 2013</Ref> Belgrano remained in Primera División after a 4–4 tie because of the sporting advantage rule, while Almagro relegated Instituto after winning 3–1 on points,<ref>[https://www.lavoz.com.ar/deportes/futbol/instituto-a-dos-decadas-de-un-golpe-tan-duro-como-inmerecido/ Instituto, a dos décadas de un golpe tan duro como inmerecido] on La Voz, 23 Jul 2020</ref> promoting to Primera División for the first time in its history.<ref>[http://almagro.club/diecinueve-anos-ascenso-inolvidable/ DIECINUEVE AÑOS DE UN ASCENSO INOLVIDABLE] on Almagro.club, 2019</ref>
